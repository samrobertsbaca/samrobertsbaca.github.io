# the price of peace

last night i dreamt i lived in a vast, open apartment high in the sky, part of a city made of connected, blocky adobe buildings. the path home wound long and slow, but the day felt gentle, unhurried. peaceful, even.

from far away, my neighbor warsby filled the air with his meditative drone frequencies under purple lights. he wasn’t meditating — he was upstairs in his private gym — but the sound claimed a spiritual presence nonetheless. what unsettled me wasn’t the noise, but the dissonance: transcendence performed without presence, calm declared without truth. it felt hollow. off.

then redsby appeared, a ghostly friend returned from long travels. familiar and dear, suddenly standing in my sunlit room. we tried to reconnect, to touch the ease of old warmth, but my mind wouldn’t stay. it kept circling stories of loss — what had changed, what had been taken, what could not be reclaimed. the more redsby tried to placate and pacify me, the more tangled i became. overwhelmed, i stepped onto the balcony to breathe. the sky offered no answers, only space.

it is very hard to play with someone you feel wronged by. without truth, intimacy becomes performance. you can follow the motions, say the right things, even touch the right places — but something essential is missing. some say there is no difference between play and performance. from the outside, perhaps there isn’t. from the inside, the difference is everything.

---

as revelations continue to surface — networks of abuse, protection, silence, and evasion — it becomes clear that this is never just about individual crimes. it is about what happens when systems violate something sacred and refuse to fully tell the truth about it. when accountability is partial, delayed, or abstracted, trust collapses. and when trust collapses, righteousness rushes in.

people moralize because it is the last remaining shelter for dignity. if no one will name the harm, at least we can be right about it. at least we can refuse to participate. this is why so many want to burn the whole system down — not because they love destruction, but because fire feels more honest than silence.

the same dynamic lives in relationships. when someone feels hurt and unheard, they stop playing and start keeping score. when a society feels betrayed by institutions that will not own their actions, cooperation collapses into outrage or withdrawal. empathy disappears — not because people are cruel, but because presence feels unsafe when truth is continually withheld.

there is a belief in certain elite circles that trauma produces strength, even liberation. to believe in an “elite” at all is to accept a story of separation: that some are fundamentally different, beyond ordinary limits, justified in extreme tests of endurance and morality. trauma becomes a tool to refine power. yet this path produces fractured, survival-minded people — capable, disciplined, and deeply disconnected from love. behavior built on this logic can no longer be excused. and institutions that continue to obscure these truths, choosing continuity over accountability, can no longer be trusted.

the fact is trauma does shape us. all of us face it, because trauma is simply the experience of disruptive change. but there is a difference between facing trauma honestly and imposing it as proof of worth. resilience born of truth tends toward integration. resilience born of denial tends toward domination.

peace is often spoken of as something soft, passive, or free. but peace has a price, and the price is truth. not punishment, not humiliation, but reality spoken plainly and unflinchingly. peace without truth is not peace — it is quiet force. it is order held together by exhaustion, fear, or performance.

from the ego’s perspective, truth feels dangerous. it threatens the narrative of being right. it asks for the surrender of grievance, not because harm didn’t occur, but because harm cannot be healed while it is denied or weaponized. what feels like annihilation to the separate self is liberation to the whole.

this is why repair matters more than reconciliation. reconciliation without truth is theater. repair requires consequence — not as retribution, but as commitment. consequence is the visible shape of responsibility. it is how truth becomes trustworthy again.

every compounding rupture narrows the margin for repair. sometimes separation is necessary. but often, separation functions as avoidance. without truth, the same patterns reappear elsewhere, wearing new faces, forming new communities, repeating the same fractures.

repair becomes possible only when belonging widens — when people are no longer treated as disposable affiliations, but as part of a shared, imperfect, collective family — even the parts that have disturbed the peace most deeply. not because harm is excused, but because real lasting justice must unite, not divide.

old guidance becomes practical here: think universally, act locally. we do not heal the whole system at once. we tell the truth where we stand. we act decently with the people in front of us. we protect our nervous systems so exhaustion is not mistaken for clarity.

in my dream, i didn’t explode or accuse. i didn’t perform peace, and i didn’t reach for fire. i stepped onto the balcony and let out a deep sigh. that gesture matters. it is the refusal to fake intimacy where truth has not yet arrived. the refusal to burn everything down just to feel righteous. it is the choice to remain present without pretending resolution.

some will say that because everything is impermanent, anything can be justified. indeed, anything is possible in this infinite universe, and perhaps life is but a dream. but the question remains: do we want to dream a world of pain or a world of peace together?

the price of peace is high in a world preoccupied with power. it costs the certainty of righteousness, the comfort of moral superiority, the illusion that being right will save us. but the alternative — endless performance, endless outrage, endless fire — costs far more.

scorsby
