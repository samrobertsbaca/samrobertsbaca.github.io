# the kim chi of life

![](/images/kimchi.png)

i've been thinking about kimchi. you know, that classic fermented cabbage that strengthens your gut microbiome. but what's actually happening inside that jar is more fascinating than most people realize — and i think it has something profound to teach us about how to live.

when you salt cabbage and pack it into an airtight container, you're creating an oxygen-free environment where wild lactobacillus bacteria — already living naturally on the cabbage leaves — begin to thrive. they consume the cabbage's natural sugars and produce lactic acid as a byproduct. that lactic acid progressively lowers the pH of the whole environment, making it too acidic for harmful bacteria to survive. the good bacteria are literally engineering their own protective ecosystem as they go.

the result is kimchi — preserved and alive for three to six months. a life-extending technology. but here's the most remarkable part: nobody is in charge. no single organism is directing the process. the lactobacillus aren't trying to preserve the kimchi. preservation is simply the byproduct of each organism fully living its own nature. the order, the longevity, the protection from decay — all of it emerges spontaneously from each component being completely itself.

it's not stasis either. it's a dynamic equilibrium. the fermentation continues slowly over months, deepening the whole time. alive and changing, yet protected from corruption by the very aliveness of it.

and when you eat kimchi, you're not just eating cabbage anymore. you're consuming that entire ecosystem — billions of living biological entities entering into relationship with your own inner ecosystem, your gut, which is itself a vast community of microorganisms maintaining its own dynamic equilibrium. in a way, to eat anything isn't really to consume — it's to combine. it's a kind of food yoga. a union between yourself and what you're taking in.

and if that's true for one body, why not for many?

what if we could live the way kimchi works? what if we learned to harmonize with each other and with the world around us so well that together we created something that outlives its individual components — a society, a collective consciousness, a world that transcends any one of us? not through control or design, but through each person simply becoming more fully themselves. because that's the paradox at the heart of it: the more deeply you know yourself, the more naturally you belong to something larger. self-knowledge isn't isolation — it's the root system that connects you to everything else.

true resilience, i think, doesn't come from eating kimchi, though it certainly doesn't hurt. it comes from aligning yourself first and foremost with truth, with light. and that alignment naturally organizes everything else — your relationships with people, with the earth, with animals and plants, with the universe and beyond. just as the lactobacillus doesn't try to preserve — it simply lives fully, and preservation follows — we don't need to try to build a better world through force or design. we simply need to deepen our own light. and the rest emerges.

by deepening our understanding of self, we deepen our relationship to all selves — of light meeting light, joyously shining on.

the true kimchi is not the kimchi which can be spoken. it's the kimchi of being light awareness.

take that as you will. in the end, it's not the words anyone says to you that matter. it's not anything outside of you. it all comes down to you. you are the wisdom you seek and you are the love you're looking for. all any of us can do is remind each other that we're on the right path — and treat each other as lovingly, and as lightly, as possible.

peace, love, and joy. always.
