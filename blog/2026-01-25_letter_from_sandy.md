Jan 1961

# letter from sandy

![](./images/50scar.png)

Dear Danny,

I’ve been thinking about the streets we raced, the sock hops we spun through, the summer nights at the Frosty Palace when the jukebox blared and we thought the world was ours. I remember your grin, your leather jacket, your thrill for chaos. And I remember me—the girl who tried for so long to hold onto you, your fire, and the music we made together.

Here’s the thing I’ve learned, Danny: love isn’t about sparks or speed or being the one everyone notices. Love is steady. It doesn’t pull people into drama to feel alive. It doesn’t ask to forgive patterns that never changed. It doesn’t need a song to announce its existence.

You’re not a suitable partner for me—not because you’re bad, but because you’re still spinning around in your saggy old wheels, still chasing attention, still using nostalgia as a charm offensive. I’m not interested in driving around in your beat-up, passed-around jalopy. I’ve built a life where my peace, my rhythm, and my boundaries are non-negotiable. I will not step off my track for someone who still treats life like a sock hop competition.

I got your record—*Greased Lightnin’*—Very cute. But a bit on the nose, don’t you think? I see even now it’s still all about you. It’s the same old story—charm, chaos, orbiting others, leaving the fallout behind. And honestly, I didn’t even listen to it… I think it would sound better on someone else’s jukebox. You already took back the axe you gave me for my birthday—you may as well keep your music too. Hope your bass playing’s improved—I’d hate for your ego to be the only thing that’s grown.

Here’s the thing: you’re not the one I want anymore. I’m not interested in revisiting old hoo-ha, being triangulated into your squad of leather-clad poseurs, or cleaning up the mess left behind by your diner dashes. My life is full. My love is measured. My friends are not props in your encore. I am not available for nostalgia, performance, or chaos disguised as desire. We did have our moment, Danny. But it’s long gone, and I’m not chasing echoes of a high school musical. As much as you like to kid yourself, it’s just not the 50’s anymore.

I wish you well Danny, sincerely. Go ahead and sing your song, but I’ll be dancing to my own drum. And the beat? It’s steady. And it’s mine alone. Best of luck with the good, bad, n’ dirty life you’ve chosen for yourself.

Bye Felicia (or whatever the kids say these days),  
Sandy
