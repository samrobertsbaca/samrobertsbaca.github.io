Oct 20

# PHỞ THE STARS

EXT. SIDE STREET – NIGHT

Rain falls like warm static. Neon hums, letters bleeding into one another.
A paper lantern floats loose from its tether, rising slowly toward the clouds.

A sign flickers:

PHỞ THE STARS — NO CREDIT. NO SIGNAL. NO REFUNDS.

INT. PHỞ THE STARS – CONTINUOUS

Steam coils through the air — half broth, half fog.
Everything glows faintly blue, as if lit by memory.

A RADIO murmurs somewhere, its signal barely clinging to the static:

RADIO (V.O.)
Tonight’s weather: soft rain, mild heartbreak.
Don’t forget to breathe between thoughts.

In the corner booth, DR. SCORSBINGTON sits alone.
He is not quite human — his features shimmer, rearranging slightly each time you look. A linen suit catches the light wrong, bending it inward.

The door chimes.
A soaked HUMAN steps in, hesitating.

He approaches.

THE HUMAN
You’re Dr. Scorsbington?

DR. SCORSBINGTON
(soft smile)
Some nights, yes. Sit.

The Human does.
Steam rises between them, forming slow, luminous shapes before vanishing.

THE HUMAN
Someone told me you help people understand... endings.

DR. SCORSBINGTON
I just listen until the soup cools.

A bowl slides onto the table by itself. The broth glows faintly. The Human stares into it — a swirling reflection of stars.

THE HUMAN
There was a girl. Anise.
We used to talk — long messages, late nights.
Then one day... nothing.
Blocked, unblocked, blocked again.

DR. SCORSBINGTON
(quietly)
Humans signal in bursts. You mistake interference for intention.

THE HUMAN
I lied to see her. Bought a ticket to a movie that didn’t exist.
It wasn’t supposed to matter, I just—
wanted to be near her.

DR. SCORSBINGTON
All planets lie a little, just to feel their own gravity.

A long silence.
Chopsticks float upright for a moment, then fall.

THE HUMAN
I tried to pray it away. But I still think of her. Every morning.

DR. SCORSBINGTON
Prayer is memory with better manners.

THE HUMAN
Do you think she still loves me?

DR. SCORSBINGTON
Love doesn’t leave. It just stops answering questions.

They eat quietly.
Outside, the rain turns to light.
The RADIO drifts through:

RADIO (V.O.)
Some storms fall upward. Don’t be alarmed.

THE HUMAN
You talk like you’ve been human.

DR. SCORSBINGTON
(looking into his bowl)
Maybe I dreamed it once.

The Human looks down — his reflection in the broth now shows two faces, overlapping. He blinks; it’s gone.

DR. SCORSBINGTON
When the taste fades, leave the spoon.
When the ache fades, leave the name.

THE HUMAN
And if it never fades?

DR. SCORSBINGTON
Then you were lucky. Some flavors never come twice.

The Human exhales — a small sound, half laugh, half sob.
He looks up.

Dr. Scorsbington is gone. Only his half-finished bowl remains, steaming faintly with starlight.

The Human stirs it once more. The broth reflects a single bright moon.

VOICEOVER (THE HUMAN)
Maybe he was just the broth thinking out loud.
Maybe I was, too.

EXT. SIDE STREET – NIGHT

The rain begins again.
The lantern returns, drifting low, brushing the puddles.

The Human steps outside and lets it pass over his head.

RADIO (V.O.)
This has been tonight’s transmission from Phở the Stars.
Whatever you lose becomes light.

FADE OUT.
