Sept. 9

# there are no waiting rooms

we are taught early to build temples and to believe that life only happens inside them.

we construct formal containers — the workroom, the stage, the sanctuary, the studio — and we save our finest posture for their doors. inside those walls, we burn our highest fuel. we speak with unarmored clarity, move with reverence, and treat every gesture as an act of sacred art. we convince ourselves that this is where the real work occurs, where our devotion is required.

then we step outside, shut the temple door, and enter what we implicitly treat as the waiting room.

in the waiting room, we default to autopilot. we treat a text message as administrative debris, a casual interaction as an obstacle to bypass, and an unscripted afternoon as an empty corridor between significant events. we flatten our voice, dim our attention, and trade the lit lamp of total presence for the cool, low-voltage ease of minimal capacity. we tell ourselves we are resting, managing our bandwidth, or saving our energy for the next structured fire.

this division is a quiet tragedy of the soul.

to treat any medium or any moment as lower ground simply because it lacks a curtain or an altar is to assume that presence is a performance rather than a state of being. it is the decision to live a compartmentalized existence — keeping the sacred locked in a professional vault while settling for a diluted life everywhere else.

**there are no waiting rooms.**

the screen in your palm is the temple floor. the three lines of text sent across a digital grid are a handwritten letter. the quiet, unscripted pause between two people in the grocery parking lot is the ceremony.

when you choose to inhabit every corner of existence with care, you are not waiting for someone to build a stage for you. you are refusing to live in fragments. it is true that this standard is rare, and that much of the world prefers the safety of the lobby, offering depth only when a role demands it and retreating into sleep when the house lights go down.

bringing full-strength attention into everyday space will inevitably confuse those who rely on compartmentalization to survive. they will look for the script, search for the container, or try to hand you a small, cool pebble to lower the temperature of the fire of your attentive presence.

let them keep their pebbles. you do not carry a lit lamp because the room requested it, or because the recipient has built a proper altar to receive it. you carry it because you have remembered that the threshold was an illusion all along.

every step is the sanctuary. every word is the song. the door was never locked, and the open field has been waiting for your feet the entire time.
