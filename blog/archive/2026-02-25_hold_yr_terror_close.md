# hold yr terror close

there's this song by the go! team called ["hold yr terror close"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vZIhNY3nqCU) which i've really enjoyed lately. there's something about a journey to the depths expressed in a deceptively cute indie-piano bop that cuts me deep. i love it. the whole song is beautiful and captures everything i'm about to say better than this essay ever will, but i wanted to at least share the chorus here:

> learning to be you is what hurts most,
> close your eyes and hold your terror close

but what i really want to talk about is **harry potter and the philosopher's stone**, for the PS1. it's a game that lives in the depths of my subconscious memory for a few standout reasons. when i was 10 years old or so, my parents bought me a playstation 1 for christmas, and got me that game with it. my mom later stated voiced her regret about this gift, as she said it led to my "being addicted to videogames" for the next decade of my life. well... yeah. in some ways i agree — maybe i would have learned how to better socialize with others at an earlier age than i did — on the other hand, videogames served as a very real surrogate digital companion of joy and discovery in a life lacking a certain emotional warmth... isn't it nice you can be radically honest about these things, and hold your terror close here on www.scorsby.us?

but on the positive side, these games didn't just give me something to do while i was kooped up in the house. they taught me valuable skills, like perseverance and fortitude, and in the case of harry potter and the sorcerer's stone, bravery.

i don't know if you've ever played the game — out of all the harry potter games ever made it's not the most well-known and highly underrated, except for maybe the [hagrid meme](https://static0.srcdn.com/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/5c7a372ce9260.jpeg?q=50&fit=crop&w=825&dpr=1.5), but make no bones about it — it's a real descent into the darkness and back. the whole game is quite eerie, from its moody textures and sounds of your feet squeaking on the floor, ghosts flying through the empty and forlorn castle, and mysterious leaky pipes and pips of your wand. it does a marvelous job with its atmosphere, and its vision of hogwarts is quite a desolate, lonesome experience of eating chocolate frogs and magic beans, jumping across chasms, unlocking large clanking doors, and saying "flipendo" more times than you can shake a wand. if you know, you know.

![](./images/potter1.png)

the game starts out spooky and by the end it's absolutely terrifying. it seriously never lets up. i remember when i first encountered the trolls in the dungeon that stomp around and moan, and not wanting to progress any further. or the part where you have to shoot down floating evil eyes that laugh maniacally. or the part where you have to sneak around in an invisibility cloak to avoid filch and his filchy cat. or the part where you have to follow the trail of unicorn's blood in the deep dark forest. and don't get me started on peeves. the endgame consists of a literal delve into the deep dungeons of hogwarts, where you have to undergo a series of increasingly demented trials and tribulations, including luring a sleepy troll into an endless pit of darkness, playing as a pawn in a chess game of life and death, and of course, fighting lo-fi voldemort on the back of professor quirrel's shrively head.

you know, reading the book and watching the movie was one thing, but actually playing as harry potter, as a ten-year-old kid — inhabiting his avatar — really made me feel like i *was* the boy who lived. for my impressionable young mind, the game wasn't just a game — it was part of the formative memory makings of my childhood. it's interesting to have grown up in the 5th generation of video game consoles, where the tech was good enough to give us abstract interactive hallucinations of the third dimension — as if to say, me and the computer grew up side by side in our understanding of the 3d world around us.

![](./images/potter2.png)

anyway, the reason i'm even talking about this game at all is that a particular scene that came to mind, in which you first enter the end-game dungeon sequence, after luring cerberus the three-headed dog to sleep. up until this point, your friends ron and hermione are right by your side, supporting you as you venture deep into the dungeons to find voldemort. but we get to this moment where before you can truly dive in, a giant spiky vine plant, known in-universe as a [devil's snare](https://harrypotter.fandom.com/wiki/Devil%27s_Snare), sprouts out of a hole in the sewers and creates a barrier of prickly vines separating you from your companions. i recall this part of the game being quite hard, shooting down the auxillery vinestalks before a giant, more menacing one branches from the center, and deals incredible damage when it slaps down on you. after beating the faceless beast, harry is reunited with his friends and is able to continue onward.

i think the devil's snare is a great analog to a certain state of consciousness we all have to face. at some point or another, we all reach a blockage (or many!) throughout the course of our lives, a giant spiky vine plant that grows out of the sewers of our awareness, that in the moment of recognizing it, separates us from everyone we love, and forces us to contend with it on our own. that's part of the deal of coming into this planet, into this veiled subset of reality where consciousness is learning to experience itself within the bounds of separation. the funny thing is, on some level we're all experiencing the same spiky nightmare in front of us, and yet we each must contend with it in such a way that it feels personal.

![](./images/potter3.png)

it says something about the nature of our relationships too — that we've all got to be willing to face the darkness, to hold our terror close, if we have any chance of actually getting closer to one another. we can comfort each other, we can be there, we can assist in so many ways, but the truth is only you can look the terror in the eyes and choose to come to terms with it or resist it, saving it for another moment when it will inevitably come back to test your readiness again. the cost in the third dimension for failing to face your fears is, of course, time. but it also impacts our ability to relate. if we don't face our devil's snares alone, we end up reacting to others through our masks or our fears, rather than responding as our true selves. this is why when you face your terror, you often experience massive shifts in your relationships, or you find that certain relationships seem to dissipate entirely — in other words, whether or not you and your other selves have both faced the terror determines the nature of how authentic of a relationship you can have with those around you.

i think that true courage comes from the clear understanding that, yes, once again, all is one. and if all is one, you're never actually alone. that the concept of aloneness doesn't actually exist because there is no separation. how many times do you have to die before you realize you're eternal? and how many giant spiky vine plants do we have to face until we realize the spiky vine plant was you all along? these are the questions and this is the journey of a lifetime. as the go! team says, [learning to be you is what hurts most. close your eyes and hold your terror close.](https://thegoteam.bandcamp.com/track/hold-yr-terror-close-the-pastels-rework)
