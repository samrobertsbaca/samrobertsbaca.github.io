# living on the edge

everything is passage. every threshold we cross, from the most intimate to the most cosmic, follows the same pattern: two things in collaboration, calling each other forward, until something that couldn't exist before suddenly does.

consider where you began. two people came together — sensation calling to attention, attention calling to sensation — building between them a positive feedback loop until the energy generated tipped paradoxically into dissolution. the french call this la petite mort, the little death, because at that moment the boundaries of the self briefly disappeared into something larger. and yet this dissolution is also called conception. something new becomes possible precisely in the dissolving. the act of union is itself a passage — two becoming one becoming three.

and if new life is conceived, it enters the next passage: the body of the mother. here is something rarely said: the fetus initiates its own birth. it is the child who sends the signal that the time has come, and the mother's body that responds. from there it is a call and response — the two of them in conversation through chemistry and pressure and rhythm, collaborating toward an opening neither could reach alone. the child is not a passenger, but a co-author of its own arrival. by the same token, the child gives birth to the mother — calling her into an identity she could not have reached without it. they make each other. the collaboration is total.

what looks like separation is actually the first real meeting. before birth, the child knew the mother only from within — felt her warmth, her rhythm, her voice moving through water. and the mother knew the child only as an interior presence, a weight, a movement, a mystery. birth is the moment they can finally see each other face to face. what the passage releases is not just a new life but a new relationship — creator and creation discovering they were always both at once.

and then the child is born into the next passage: a body, a family, a culture, a world. each one warm and necessary and, in time, exactly the right size to have outgrown. the shell doesn't become smaller. you become larger than what can hold you. and so you press, from the inside, not in opposition but in the same collaborative spirit — feeling the walls not as enemies but as the very resistance that has been shaping you, the way the egg shapes the bird, the way the riverbed shapes the river.

death, too, is passage. the body releases what has been forming. and the soul moves through into something we don't yet have words for. the little death and the large death and every threshold in between are the same movement at different scales. each release is also an arrival. each ending is a collaboration between what is being let go and what is doing the letting.

whatever you are, beneath all the forms that have held you, you are not something that passes through things. you are the passage itself. not the water, not the river, not even the flowing — but the fact that something is always, joyfully, wondrously, inexplicably moving through.

and we are doing this together. if we are the fetus in the womb of collective consciousness, then we are not waiting to be born. we are already sending the signal. our longing, our art, our reaching toward something more than what this moment can contain — these are the messages that initiate our mother's labor. we are actively collaborating with the world that holds us, calling it toward an opening it cannot reach without us, just as it is calling us toward an opening we cannot reach without it.

every origin traces back through mothers and mothers and mothers to a single source. which means none of us are truly foreign to each other. which means the word alien may describe something that does not actually exist. we are all held in the same passage — the same endless, luminous, collaborative moving through — living on the edge of remembering that we are the edge itself.
